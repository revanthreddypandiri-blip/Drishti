Most AV stacks ask “where is the lane?”

On a village road, in a market, at an unsignalled junction — there is no lane. There are autos, two-wheelers, pushcarts, pedestrians and cattle, all reading each other in real time. Planning around them one by one is how you get surprised. DRISHTI models the interaction.

The problem statement

Adaptive path planning for autonomous vehicles in unstructured, mixed Indian traffic. Build a planner that stays safe and keeps moving where lane discipline is weak, signals are absent, and every road user reacts to every other.

Gap 01

No lanes to follow

Faded, broken or missing markings and uneven edges break lane-keeping planners. The drivable space has to be inferred, not read.

Gap 02

Mixed, vulnerable users

Autos, two-wheelers, carts, pedestrians and cattle share one carriageway at very different speeds and sizes.

Gap 03

Informal negotiation

Junctions have no signals and no enforced priority. Merging and crossing are settled by eye contact and nerve.

Gap 04

Chain reactions

One swerve becomes many. Treating agents as independent obstacles means the planner is always one step late.

What the PS asks for

Real-time replanning with probabilistic intent prediction, multi-cause uncertainty, occlusion handling, a negotiation-aware behavior layer and an independent safety verifier, validated across five scenarios: village road, urban junction, highway merge, market and cattle crossing.

01 / 05

A pothole appears

A static hazard mid-road. Nothing has reacted yet — a conventional planner sees only an obstacle it isn't near.

02 / 05

The auto swerves

The first reaction. DRISHTI detects the swerve as a root event and injects it into the interaction graph.

03 / 05

The two-wheeler reacts

The ripple propagates hop by hop — delayed by reaction time, decaying with distance.

04 / 05

The pedestrian hesitates

Behavior uncertainty rises. Their safety buffer widens before they do anything unexpected.

05 / 05

Ego slows — early

The corridor narrows, risk budget tightens, and DRISHTI eases off before the final conflict develops.
