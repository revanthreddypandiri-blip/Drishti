Smart India Hackathon · Prototype

Indian roads don't have obstacles.
They have negotiations.

DRISHTI is an adaptive path-planning system for autonomous vehicles on unstructured Indian roads. Instead of treating every road user as an object to avoid, it models traffic as a web of agents who react to each other — and gives the ego vehicle the ability to anticipate that reaction chain before it reaches its own corridor.

01 The core idea: Traffic Ripple Prediction

A pothole doesn't just sit there. It causes an auto to swerve. The swerve causes a following two-wheeler to react. The two-wheeler's sudden move makes a nearby pedestrian hesitate. By the time any of this reaches the ego vehicle's own lane, it's already a three-hop chain reaction — and a system that only reasons about the immediate obstacle sees none of it coming.

Pothole
→
Auto swerves
→
Two-wheeler reacts
→
Pedestrian hesitates
→
Ego slows down

DRISHTI builds a dynamic interaction graph every tick — every nearby agent is a node, proximity-weighted edges connect them — and propagates detected root events (a hard brake, a swerve) outward through that graph as a delayed, decaying reaction, up to 3 hops. It's rule-based and fully inspectable, not a black-box trajectory predictor: you can point at any reaction and trace exactly which upstream event caused it.

02 Negotiation-aware planning, not obstacle→STOP

A human driver doesn't slam the brakes for every auto approaching from the side — they slow slightly, hold a buffer, and negotiate the shared space. DRISHTI makes this explicit as a five-state behavior machine, driven by a live Traffic Stress Index:

StressStateBehavior
0–20FOLLOWNormal trajectory
20–40CAUTIOUSIncreased clearance around nearby agents
40–60NEGOTIATEReduced speed, actively negotiate the shared corridor
60–80YIELDConservative replanning — give way rather than contest it
80–100EMERGENCYYield/stop; Safety Shield is final authority

Critically, the state doesn't just label the decision — it biases the planner's own cost function (a real, visible cost penalty on maneuvers the state doesn't prefer), so the relationship between "environment becomes chaotic" and "vehicle behavior changes" is an actual algorithmic dependency, not dashboard decoration.

Risk Budget ≠ Traffic Stress Index

Stress describes the environment (how chaotic is it right now). Risk Budget is the planner's allowable exposure (100 − stress, non-linearly) — it directly scales how risk-averse the cost function is this tick. Two distinct, connected quantities, not one gauge doing two jobs.

03 A drivable corridor, not a lane

Most of the road network described in the problem statement has no painted lane markings. So DRISHTI never asks "where is the lane" — it asks "what region of the road is safely drivable right now?", computed fresh every tick from the road boundary, static obstacles, nearby agents (each widened by its own uncertainty), producing a continuously reshaping corridor that the planner's lateral maneuvers are clamped to.

04 Uncertainty has causes, not just magnitude

Instead of one arbitrary safety-margin fudge factor, every perceived agent carries a four-part uncertainty breakdown, so the planner can react to why something is uncertain:

Behavior uncertainty

How spread-out its intent-hypothesis distribution is, plus a live surprise score — how much its actual motion deviates from a simple extrapolation of its last observed state.

Perception uncertainty

1 − sensor confidence for this detection: range, field-of-view, and current lighting/fog conditions all feed this.

Occlusion uncertainty

How long it's been since this agent was actually observed rather than inferred — a confidence that decays the longer an agent stays hidden.

Environment uncertainty

How locally unclear the road boundary itself is (a village road's edge can change width meter to meter).

These combine into a single multiplier that widens that agent's effective safety margin — a pedestrian with high behavior uncertainty gets a bigger buffer than one with low uncertainty, even at the same distance.

05 Occlusion-aware hidden-agent risk

A bus or truck doesn't just occlude whatever happens to be standing behind it in this particular run — it creates a standing possibility that something is there, in every run. DRISHTI computes an occlusion zone behind every large vehicle within sensor range, and treats entering that zone at speed as a real (small) cost, whether or not an agent actually materializes there — "potential unseen road user" rather than "nothing detected, continue."

06 Behavior profiles, not just object categories

Every perceived agent is classified by behavior, not only kind: Stable, Cautious, Erratic, Crossing, Filtering, Oncoming, Stationary, or Occluded. An auto-rickshaw that's Filtering through traffic is planned around differently than one that's Stable — the planner responds to what the agent is doing, not just what it is.

07 Adaptive sensor trust

Camera, LiDAR and radar aren't blended equally in every condition. Under low light or fog, camera confidence drops and the fused detection leans on LiDAR/radar; a fast-moving agent gets a radar-confidence boost. The planner doesn't just get "there is an object" — it gets "there is an object, and here is our current confidence in its position and motion," which feeds directly into that agent's perception-uncertainty term.

08 Explainable, every tick

Every decision the ego makes produces a structured, human-readable trace: the reason, a 0–100 risk score, the action taken, and the next re-evaluation interval — visible live in the simulation, and traceable back through the ripple graph when the cause is a chain reaction rather than a direct threat. This is also the audit trail a regulator, insurer, or post-incident investigator would actually need — a black-box neural trajectory predictor can't produce this.

09 Independent Safety Shield

The planner's chosen trajectory is re-checked by a deliberately simpler, independent module: no intent hypotheses, no ripple graph — just a conservative constant-velocity worst-case extrapolation of every currently perceived agent. If that ever predicts a closer pass than a hard safety margin allows, the Shield vetoes the planner and substitutes an emergency-braking fallback. Two independent lines of defense, on purpose.

10 Why this is deployable, not just a demo

The full architecture above is rule-based and probabilistic rather than a GPU-hungry deep perception/prediction pipeline, so it runs on embedded-class compute (no automotive LiDAR required) — positioning DRISHTI as a low-cost retrofit safety layer for existing Indian fleet vehicles, not a decade-away full self-driving stack. See the cost comparison in the project's docs/ folder for real, cited hardware figures.

Every run is seeded and procedurally varied — scenario layouts, agent counts, and even lighting/fog conditions are generated from the seed rather than hardcoded, so the same scenario key produces a genuinely different situation each time while keeping its defining character intact.
